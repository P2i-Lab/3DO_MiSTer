# Measurements of a real 3DO (Panasonic FZ-10) for the MiSTer core

Between 9 and 14 November 2026 we ran a homebrew benchmark disc, P2i Lab bench (versions 2.3 to 3.6), on a Japanese Panasonic FZ-10 (NTSC). The disc measures what the console does: cel engine times, matrix math, ARM behaviour, memory and video timing, the CD drive, the control port and the light gun. This folder holds everything we read on the console, with our reading of it and the comparison with our simulated test cores.

It is meant as documentation. The core changes we made from these values are on our fork, for reference only.

## What is here

- `data/disc-X.Y/`: the values read on the FZ-10, one file per page or group of pages, translated from our French notes. Every number was checked against the original notes by script: the same digit groups in the same order. Most files end with a "Reading:" paragraph, which is our interpretation.
- `data/disc-3.6/comparaison-coeur-*.txt` and similar files: our simulated test cores against the FZ-10 values.
- `disc/`: the sources of the bench program. The disc image is attached to the release (see "The disc").

The file names are in French (they follow the pages of the disc), and the contents are in English. Pages of discs 2.3 to 3.5 have French titles on screen; each file keeps the title and adds an English gloss, e.g. CELS BALAYAGE (cel sweep). Some files keep French decimal commas: 0,94 means 0.94. Some files name our simulation runs or scripts (paths such as `/root/devkit/...` or `scratchpad ...`): they are kept to show where a figure comes from; those tools are not published.

## How the values were taken

- The bench runs as an ordinary application under Portfolio OS, launched from the devkit's menu.
- Times are microseconds from `SampleSystemTimeTV()`, which advances in steps of 16 us on the console. VBL counts come from the timer device.
- Cel times are medians of 5 or 9 draws of the same list. From disc 3.6 on, each draw starts right after a wake-up on a VBL.
- Each page shows grids of values, labelled like F0..FF or R0..R7. We photographed the screen and transcribed the photos by hand, and later with an OCR made for the bench font. Doubtful digits were checked again on the screen. The photos are not published.
- Core figures come from Verilator simulations of our test cores, unless a file says "real MiSTer". The simulations use the same disc image and the same pad presses. Some files also give Opera (libretro) for reference.
- One console, one drive, our CD-Rs. CD figures that depend on the disc (rotation times) hold for our CD-Rs.

## The disc

- Disc 3.6 image: `helloworld.iso`, 146 706 432 bytes, SHA-1 `cddbc915b8a26ea5286e6c53bf505fc4869e455c`, attached to the release [bench-disc-3.6](https://github.com/P2i-Lab/3DO_MiSTer/releases/tag/bench-disc-3.6). It is the image the disc 3.6 values come from.
- It is built with trapexit's 3do-devkit (https://github.com/trapexit/3do-devkit): copy `disc/src/bench` into the devkit's `src/` and run `make`. The texts are English by default, French with `BENCH_FR`.
- The image also holds the devkit's own examples and system files. The bench's pictures are generated by the program. Its two CD test files (`bench_data/cd512k.bin`, 512 KB, and `pad.bin`, 128 MB) are simple generated patterns; take them from the image to rebuild it. There is no game data. The Strahl-like cels only copy the formats (CCB flags, sizes, packing) of the two cels of Strahl's intro slide show.
- To run it: choose P2I LAB BENCH in the devkit menu. The bench menu lists the groups of tests; ALL 3.6 A and ALL 3.6 B run the pages new in 3.6.

## Main findings

Each point gives the file it comes from; the file has the full figures.

### Cel engine

- **ACE.** With CCB ACE clear the console draws with one corner engine. The cores we started from always used both. A Strahl-like loop (a full-screen fill, then a 320x240 packed cel) takes 120 VBL on the console and took 60 on the core (disc-2.3, BOUCLE).
- **ACE and the vertical scale.** ACE saves 19 to 31 % when the vertical scale is at most 1, and only 2 to 6 % between 1.1 and 1.7. Part of the gain comes back at 2 (17 %), 2.5 (17 %) and 3 (34 %). A horizontal scale above 1 does not stop the two engines (disc-3.3/p02).
- **ACE at 90 degrees.** Rotated by 90 degrees, ACE saves almost nothing: 8 bits at scale 1, 6720 us without ACE and 6544 with it, against 5888 and 4448 at 0 degrees (disc-3.6, grid R).
- **Source fetch by groups of four words.** The time per row grows by about 0.08 us per pixel without ACE, with two steps of 0.5 to 0.8 us after each group of four source words (disc-3.3/p03-p05). The groups start at the row, not at a 16-byte boundary. A source in VRAM costs the same as in DRAM (disc-3.4/p02). A cel of one row takes the same time with and without ACE (disc-3.3/p05).
- **Opaque and transparent pixels.** In ns per source pixel, all opaque / all transparent: 6 bits without ACE 141 / 87, with ACE 91 / 43; 16 bits without ACE 212 / 118, with ACE 148 / 71 (disc-3.3/p06). With ACE a transparent packed run costs half the time it costs without ACE, whatever its length. LCE makes opaque cels 3 to 14 % faster than ACE alone (disc-3.4/p03).
- **Pixels outside the screen.** They still cost time: 218 ns per source pixel at scale 1.41 without ACE, and about 106 ns at scale 1 (disc-3.3/p01).
- **Blends.** A half blend adds 150 to 173 ns per screen pixel without ACE, and 124 to 158 ns with ACE (disc-3.3/p02).
- **Shifted rows.** Rows shifted by up to 1 pixel per row cost the same as straight rows. At 2 pixels per row the cel costs 26 % more (16 bits) and 83 % more (6 bits) without ACE (disc-3.4/p01).
- **Black with NOBLK clear.** On the RENDU DETAIL page, the hashes of the 8-bit uncoded draws with BGND set and NOBLK clear match the FZ-10 only when a black result is written as 0x0400. The FZ-10 hashes are in disc-3.5/p04; the variants were tried in our simulator.

### Matrix math

- **DIVZ.** The console brings the 64-bit quotient N / Z to 16.16 by truncating toward zero. Taking bits [47:16] rounds toward minus infinity, which is wrong for a negative Z. We found the rule offline: out of 327 888 rules tried, only this form gives the console's CRC. Disc 3.0's MATH EXTREMES page then confirmed it with six more CRCs (disc-2.3, "DIVZ").

### ARM CPU

- **Unaligned word accesses.** A word access at an address that is not word aligned stops the task on the FZ-10: load, store, block transfer and swap, in DRAM and in VRAM. The MADAM abort cause is ABT_BWACC, "byte address, word access". Byte accesses work at any address. Opera and the core do not raise this abort (disc-3.3/p11, disc-3.2/p05).
- **MULS and the C flag.** MULS changes C. With the rule of disc-3.3/p07-09, the CRC of our MIX family of random programs is EE219326, as on the console. When MULS keeps C, as in Opera, it is 469F96CC (disc-3.3/p10).
- Instruction times are in ARM TEMPS and memory access times in MEMOIRE PAS (disc-3.0).

### Video and system timing

- **Line timing.** 1592 clocks per line. Fields of 262 and 263 lines, line numbers starting at 4 (disc-3.1, VIDEO LIGNES).
- **Control port timing.** The control port transfer, and the system task it wakes (about 630 us together), run about 4 lines later on the FZ-10 than on our core with a pad alone, and 5 to 7 lines later with one or two guns (disc-3.5/p01 and p07, disc-3.4/p05).

### CD drive

- **Speed.** Without a speed option a read is at double speed: 6.69 to 6.72 ms per block, against 13.37 ms at 1X. The status byte is E3 at double speed and E1 at single speed (disc-3.2/p04).
- **Seeks.** With T = 6688 us per block at 2X, a step of g blocks costs g x T + n x R. R is one turn of the disc at that place: 68.7 ms at block 5000, 79.6 ms at 35000, 89.1 ms at 65000, with R^2 = 4452 + 0.0536 x block in ms^2 on this CD-R. The minimum is below 33.5 ms for steps of 5 blocks, and about 37 to 45 ms when the head must change track (disc-3.1/p06-08).
- **Through the file system.** Jumps of 2000 blocks take 87 to 205 ms (median 146 ms). The first file open after an idle period takes 1.33 s: the drive, or its driver, needs about 1.2 s to wake up (disc-3.5/p03).

### Light gun (control port device 0x4D)

- **Data.** A gun answers with 5 bytes: 0x4D, the buttons (80 trigger; 48, bits 40 and 08, the calibration button), a 20-bit counter, and a 5-bit count of the lines it saw (disc-3.4/p06 and p09).
- **Counter.** It runs at 20 MHz in step with the picture. Aimed shots group best at 1271.09 counts per line (disc-3.4/p08). A bar swept under a gun at rest gives 1270.4 +- 0.2 counts per line, and 3.19 counts per pixel (disc-3.5/p06).
- **Chain.** In our set-up the guns come first on the chain and the pad after them: the pad's bytes follow the gun's 5 bytes (disc-3.4/p09). Each gun makes the transfer longer. The task at its end comes 7.5 lines later with one gun and 4.5 lines more with two, about 6 lines per gun, where 40 bits at 8 bits a line would give 5 (disc-3.5/p07).

## Our test cores against these values

The comparison files give the cores' values beside the FZ-10's, page by page. On disc 3.6 (1690 cel times), 1302 times are within 10 % of the FZ-10 on our core 1113, and 1323 on 1114 (simulated build x17c). The mean gap is 7.5 % and 7.1 %. Times more than 10 % too fast drop from 70 to 48 (`disc-3.6/comparaison-coeur-1113.txt` and `disc-3.6/comparaison-coeur-1114.txt`).

Still clearly off on our cores: some reduced and rotated cels, narrow cels with ACE, and the control port timing above.

These are our cores, not yours. The figures say where the console differs from a model, not how your core behaves.

## Not measured

- Only an NTSC console: no PAL figures.
- The DSP only partly (disc-3.0/p10).
- The light gun's own latency: counter values depend on where its sensor triggers.
